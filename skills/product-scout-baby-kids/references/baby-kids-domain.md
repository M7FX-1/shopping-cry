## Car-seat standards decode `[S]`

| Aspect | UN R129 (i-Size, 2013→) | ECE R44/04 (older) |
|---|---|---|
| Grading | child HEIGHT | child WEIGHT (groups 0/0+/I/II/III) |
| Rear-facing | mandatory to ≥15 months (~76 cm) | allowed forward-facing from ~9 kg |
| Side impact | mandatory test | not tested |
| New approvals | current standard | ended — no new R44 certifications |
| Label | orange approval label, "i-Size"/R-number, height range | orange label, R44/04 + group |

Sources: Maxi-Cosi R44-vs-R129 explainer; Swandoo regulation guide;
Bebelux comparison `[S]` (re-verify at dispatch). Buying order:
R129 > R44/04 new-with-label > never unmarked.

## Car-seat second-hand checklist (NHTSA-based) `[S]`

All must hold before a second-hand seat is even considered:
1. No moderate/severe crash history the seller can stand behind
   (NHTSA: replace after moderate/severe; minor crash only if all five
   conditions held — drivable away, nearest door undamaged, no
   injuries, no airbag deployment, no visible seat damage).
2. Manufacture-date label present and NOT past the seat's expiry
   (typical ~6–10 years; the manufacturer's stamped label wins `[P]`).
3. Model number + instructions available (needed for recall checks —
   the manufacturer's recall page by model; NHTSA for US-sold models).
4. All parts present, harness original, no visible cracks/repairs.
5. Never "expired", never "unknown history" — the two honest rejections.

## Cost-per-month worked math `[P]`

- Price per diaper = pack price ÷ pack count (net count, not "up to").
- Example: pack A EGP 260 / 60 diapers = EGP 4.33; pack B EGP 540 /
  150 = EGP 3.60. At 8 diapers/day: A ≈ EGP 1,040/month; B ≈ EGP 864/month.
- But absorption reality: if B leaks and needs a 30% change-rate bump,
  the math flips — verified leak reports weigh as much as the unit
  price. Night diapers are a separate use case (longer window).
- Bulk rule: only in a settled size; newborn/small sizes are
  outgrown faster than packs empty.
- Local shelf anchors `[P 2026-11]`: Pampers (premium
  tier), Molfix and Baby Care (value tier); the core's cheaper-
  alternative step usually resolves across these three.
- Wipes: price per wipe = pack price ÷ count; the true cost line is
  wipes-per-change (thin wipes come 2-per-change).

## WHO/CDC formula windows `[S]`

| Situation | Limit | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Water temperature for powdered formula | ≥70 °C, then cool quickly | WHO 2007 guidance; CDC `[S]` |
| Room-temperature storage (prepared) | use within 2 h | CDC `[S]` |
| Refrigerated (prepared, immediately chilled) | use within 24 h | CDC `[S]` |
| Started feed | finish within 1 h, discard the rest | CDC `[S]` |
| Microwave reheating | never (uneven hot spots) | standard-safety `[P]` |
| Honey under 1 year | never (infant botulism) | health-skill rail, restated `[S]` |

Reason the rail exists: Cronobacter — powdered formula is not sterile.

## Nipple-flow starting bands `[P]`

| Band | Rough age | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| Preemie/0 | preterm/first weeks | extra-slow |
| Slow (1) | ~0–3 months | many babies stay here for months |
| Medium (2) | ~3–6 months | brand-dependent — bands differ per brand |
| Fast (3) | ~6–12 months | advance on cues, not the calendar |

No industry flow-rate standard exists; drip rates vary widely between
brands (Nationwide Children's, Evenflo, Pados 2016 `[S]`). Cues rule:
gulping/choking/leaking = too fast; exhaustion mid-feed = too slow.

## Crib standards detail `[S]`

- 16 CFR 1219 (full-size) / 1220 (non-full-size), incorporating
  ASTM F1169/F406 — adopted Dec 2010, effective 2011; all cribs
  (including childcare/hotel use) must comply.
- Slat spacing ≤ 2⅜ in (6 cm); corner posts ≤ 1/16 in; mattress gap
  ≤ two fingers (CPSC crib-safety guidance + AAP).
- Drop-side designs: effectively banned by the 2011 standard
  requirements.
- Safe-sleep product rejects for under-1 sleep spaces (AAP-class
  guidance): pillows, quilts, bumpers, positioners, weighted
  blankets, loose plush in the crib.

## Heat-care notes (Egypt lens) `[P]`

- Parked-car interior temperatures become lethal fast — the car-seat
  verdict repeats the never-parked-car rule.
- Covered strollers trap heat; mesh airflow covers are the safe
  cover; check-touch metal parts (buckles, frame) before placing the
  child.
- Baby skincare in dry heat: fragrance-free moisturizer after bath;
  sun = shade, hats, timing — sunscreen product choice cross-refs the
  beauty domain's ingredient discipline.
- Hydration, fever, heat-rash questions are health territory — route,
  never advise.
