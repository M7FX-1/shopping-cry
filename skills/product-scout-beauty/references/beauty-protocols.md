## 1. Dandruff & Seborrheic Dermatitis (scalp)

### A. Plain language
Dandruff is accelerated scalp flaking driven by *Malassezia* yeast (lipid-dependent, overgrows in sebum-rich skin); susceptible people react to its oleic-acid metabolites, speeding up cell turnover so flakes clump and shed [S13][S14]. Seborrheic dermatitis (SD) is the same process with visible inflammation — redness, itching, yellow greasy scale — often extending to eyebrows, nasolabial folds, ears [S13][S14]. **Chronicity: not curable — managed.** It is a chronic, relapsing, waxing-and-waning condition (often worse in winter); flaking returns within ~3–8 weeks after treatment stops [S14][S19].

### B. OTC treatment ladder (antifungal shampoo classes)
| Active | Mechanism | Evidence | Concentration | Vehicle / formulation notes | Price tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zinc pyrithione (ZPT) | Antifungal/antibacterial; lowers *Malassezia* load | Strong (decades of RCTs; US OTC monograph GRASE) [S11] | 0.3–2% (US M032; 1% most common) | Rinse-off shampoo; contact time matters more than brand | Budget (store brands) to mid |
| Ketoconazole | Azole antifungal; kills *Malassezia*, anti-inflammatory | Strong (clearing + prophylaxis RCTs; fewest relapses, ~23% vs comparators) [S12][S20] | 1% OTC in US; 2% is pharmacy-sale (P) OTC in UK but Rx-only in US — **market filter required** [S45][S46] | Shampoo; label-typical contact 3–5 min `⚠ verify per product label` | Mid (branded) / generic equivalents |
| Selenium sulfide | Antifungal + slows keratinocyte turnover (anti-squaming) | Strong (head-to-head RCT vs ketoconazole 2%: both effective) [S21 via S12] | 1% US OTC (2.5% higher-strength/Rx-like in many markets) [S12] | Shampoo; **leave on 2–3 min** then rinse and repeat; 2x/week initially [S12][S13] | Budget to mid |
| Piroctone olamine | Antifungal (alternative to ZPT); gentler on hair | Moderate (0.5% comparable to ZPT 1% in controlled studies) [S15] | 0.5% typical | Shampoo; common in EU post-ZPT-ban formulas and sulfate-free systems [S15][S16] | Mid–derm-brand |
| Coal tar | Slows keratinocyte proliferation (antifungal weak) | Weak–moderate (Cochrane: no support for first-line tar shampoo in scalp psoriasis; limited dandruff data) [S17][S18] | 0.5–5% (US M032) | Shampoo; leave on ~5 min; odor/staining; photosensitivity caution [S11] | Budget to mid |
| Salicylic acid | Keratolytic; removes scale (adjunct, not antifungal) | Weak as monotherapy (Cochrane: no added benefit confirmed) [S17] | 1.8–3% (US M032) | Shampoo/descaler; useful for thick scale pre-treatment [S11][S18] | Budget |

**Scalp psoriasis overlap:** thick, well-demarcated silvery plaques need medical care; OTC tar/salicylic shampoos are adjunct descalers only — strongest evidence for scalp psoriasis is prescription potent topical steroids (clobetasol) [S17][S18].

### C. Full usage protocol
- **Active phase:** antifungal shampoo 2x/week (ketoconazole 2% label: twice weekly 3–5 min; selenium sulfide 1%: twice weekly, 2–3 min contact, rinse and repeat) [S12][S13]. `⚠ label-typical contact times — verify per product.`
- **Contact time is the dose:** lather, leave 3–5 min (selenium sulfide 2–3 min), then rinse; daily gentle cosmetic shampoo between medicated washes is fine [S12][S13].
- **Rotation strategy:** rotate two antifungal classes (e.g., ketoconazole 2x/week + ZPT or piroctone-olamine shampoo for other washes) during the active phase; switching single-agent monotherapy for rotation is standard practice to limit tachyphylaxis/relapse [S12][S14]. `⚠ rotation benefit is expert consensus, not RCT-proven.`
- **Combine with:** conditioner on lengths/ends only (not scalp); barrier care for any scalp irritation.
- **Separate from:** don't stack multiple keratolytics (SA + tar same wash) unless product is formulated as a combination.

### D. Realistic timeline
- First flaking reduction: **2–4 weeks** of correct 2x/week use; clearing typically ~4 weeks [S12][S14].
- **Relapse expectation:** after full discontinuation, relapse commonly at **3–8 weeks** [S19]; ketoconazole 2% with maintenance showed ~23–31% relapse over 2–26 week follow-ups [S12][S47 ⚠].

### E. Maintenance plan (chronic — required)
After clearing: **once weekly** ketoconazole (or 2x/week alternating antifungal classes) indefinitely or seasonally; resume 2x/week at first sign of flaking. Twice-weekly proactive antifungal maintenance significantly delays relapse vs. stopping [S12][S19][S48 ⚠].

### F. Failure / escalation
- **Refer if:** no improvement after **4–6 weeks** of correct 2x/week medicated use; immediate worsening; scalp pain/oozing.
- **Red flags (immediate referral):** thick silvery plaques extending beyond hairline (psoriasis) [S17][S18]; patchy hair loss with broken hairs/black dots (tinea capitis — needs oral antifungal) [S13]; pustules, crusting, fever, tender lymph nodes (bacterial infection) [S13].

## 2. Acne (teen + adult; comedonal to mild-moderate inflammatory)

### A. Plain language
Acne = clogged follicles (comedones) + *C. acnes* overgrowth + inflammation [S1]. Ranges from blackheads/whiteheads (comedonal) to papules/pustules (inflammatory). **Chronicity: chronic, relapsing even in adults — managed, not cured**; treatment is maintenance until the skin stops producing problems [S1].

### B. OTC treatment ladder
| Active | Mechanism | Evidence | Concentration | Vehicle / formulation notes | Price tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Benzoyl peroxide (BP) | Bactericidal to *C. acnes*; anti-inflammatory; no resistance | **Strong** (AAD 2024 strong recommendation) [S1]; 2.5% = 5% = 10% efficacy with less irritation [S4] | 2.5% start (FDA monograph 2.5–10%) [S7] | Leave-on gel/cream thin layer full-face; or 4–10% wash for body; **bleaches fabrics/towels** [S1] | Budget (generics) |
| Adapalene | Third-gen topical retinoid: comedolytic + anti-inflammatory; normalizes follicular keratinization | **Strong** (AAD strong rec; as effective as tretinoin 0.025% with faster onset + better tolerability) [S1][S5] | 0.1% gel (OTC in US since 2016) [S6] | Pea-sized amount, thin layer, entire affected face, once daily PM; buffered with moisturizer; **purge expected** [S5][S6] | Budget–mid (generics OTC) |
| Salicylic acid | Lipophilic keratolytic; unclogs pore | Moderate–weak (FDA GRASE 0.5–2% [S7]; ~20–35% lesion reduction at 12 wks; one 12-wk trial comparable to BP with better tolerability [S8]; AAD ranks below BP/retinoids) [S1] | 0.5–2% | Cleansers/leave-ons; pH-adjusted acidic formulas | Budget |
| Niacinamide | Anti-inflammatory; reduces sebum; inhibits melanosome transfer | Weak–moderate (2–4% gel ≈ clindamycin 1% in vehicle-controlled trials; AAD adjunctive) [S9] | 2–4% (products often 5%) | Leave-on serum; well tolerated, pairs with everything [S9] | Mid–derm-brand |
| Azelaic acid | Mild comedolytic, antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, anti-pigment | Weak at OTC strength (10% is cosmetic-tier; validated Rx strengths are 15%/20%) [S24] | 10% OTC | Cream/suspension; useful when acne + post-acne dark marks coexist [S24] | Mid–derm-brand |

### C. Full usage protocol (routine assembly)
- **AM:** gentle cleanser (fingertips, non-abrasive) → non-comedogenic moisturizer → SPF 30+ [S2].
- **PM:** gentle cleanser → BP 2.5% thin layer over entire acne-prone area (not spot-only) → moisturizer; introduce **3 nights/week → nightly as tolerated** [S2][S4].
- **Adding adapalene:** start every-other-night, pea-sized for whole face, then nightly in 2–4 weeks; can layer moisturizer before ("buffering") to cut irritation; BP + adapalene can be used together (validated fixed-dose combo exists) but sensitive skin should alternate nights (BP AM, adapalene PM) [S5][S6].
- **Separate:** adapalene + strong AHAs/BHAs same night (irritation stack); avoid scrubs/abrasive exfoliants and drying alcohols [S1][S2].
- **Non-negotiables:** treat the whole area, not spots; shampoo regularly if oily hair; non-comedogenic everything [S2].

### D. Realistic timeline
- First improvement: **6–8 weeks** [S3].
- Full effect: **~12 weeks (3–4 months)** of consistent daily use [S3][S5].
- **Purging vs breakout:** adapalene-driven microcomedone purge = more small inflammatory lesions in weeks 2–6 in already-acne-prone zones, stabilizing after ~6–8 weeks — do not stop [S5]. **Failure pattern instead:** new lesion types, spread beyond original zones, no stabilization by week 8–12.

### E. Maintenance plan (chronic)
Continue adapalene nightly long-term (maintenance superiority shown to 16 weeks and beyond) [S5]; BP 2–3 nights/week or as wash; acne recurs when treatment stops [S1][S5].

### F. Failure / escalation
- **Refer if:** no improvement after **12 weeks** of correctly assembled OTC routine; or deterioration after 6–8 weeks [S3].
- **Red flags (immediate referral):** nodules/cysts (scarring risk), any early scarring, pigment changes in darker skin types, sudden severe nodulocystic flare with fever/systemic symptoms (suspect acne fulminans) [S1][S3].

## 3. Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH) & Mild Melasma Support — OTC-safe tier

### A. Plain language
PIH is pigment left behind after inflammation (acne, eczema, injury) — brown in epidermis, gray-brown if deeper; it fades slowly and can be hastened by treatment [S24]. Melasma is a light/hormone-driven patchy pigmentation on the face. **Chronicity: melasma is chronic, relapsing, and has no curative treatment** — control depends on lifelong photoprotection [S29]. PIH resolves on its own but takes months [S24].

### B. OTC treatment ladder (foundation first)
| Tier | Active | Mechanism | Evidence | Concentration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Foundation** | Tinted mineral sunscreen with **iron oxides** | Blocks UV **+ visible light** (VL drives/maintains pigment, esp. darker skin) | Strong (tinted iron-oxide SPF significantly reduced melasma recurrence vs untinted; iron-oxide formulas outperform untinted mineral vs VL-induced darkening) [S22] | SPF 30–50+ | Untinted mineral SPF does not block VL; "tinted" must mean iron oxides/pigmentary TiO2 [S22] |
| 1 | Azelaic acid | Tyrosinase inhibitor, anti-inflammatory | Moderate (20% cream RCT evidence for PIH; ~71% melasma area reduction reported in reviews; **10% OTC is the weaker cosmetic tier**) [S24] | 10% OTC (15%/20% = Rx in US) | First-choice OTC brightener; pregnancy-compatible |
| 1 | Niacinamide 4% | Inhibits melanosome transfer | Moderate (4% ≈ hydroquinone 4% in a double-blind melasma RCT) [S23] | 2–4% | Best-tolerated; pairs with everything [S23] |
| 2 | Vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid) | Tyrosinase copper-site inhibition + antioxidant | Weak–moderate (5% ascorbic beat vehicle but lost to HQ 4% in RCT; systematic review: effective but few trials) [S25][S26] | 5–20%; pH < ~3.8 for L-AA `⚠ pH per formulation` | Adjunct, not monotherapy; packaging = opaque airless |
| 2 | Glycolic acid (AHA) | Exfoliation accelerates pigment shedding | Moderate (glycolic + retinol most effective for texture/pigment in comparative analyses) [S27][S28] | 5–10% leave-on | Increases sun sensitivity — only under SPF |
| 2 | Retinol | Speeds turnover, mild tyrosinase effect | Moderate (comparative effectiveness; slower/weaker than Rx tretinoin) [S27] | 0.1–0.5% cosmetic | Introduce 2–3 nights/week |

### C. Full usage protocol
- **AM:** gentle cleanser → vitamin C serum → moisturizer → **tinted iron-oxide SPF 50+**, reapplied midday [S22].
- **PM:** alternate nights: azelaic 10% OR retinol (never both same night); niacinamide 4% can be layered any night.
- **Combine:** niacinamide + azelaic; vitamin C AM under SPF.
- **Separate:** strong AHA and retinol same night (irritation → *causes* PIH); any new irritation stops the protocol (PIH is inflammation-fed).

### D. Realistic timeline
- Epidermal PIH: visible fade in **3–6 months** of strict daily SPF + actives [S24][S27].
- Melasma: meaningful change at **6+ months**; pigment often returns with sun exposure or treatment stops [S29].

### E. Maintenance plan (chronic)
Year-round tinted iron-oxide SPF + hats/shade; actives taper to 2–3 nights/week after clearance [S22][S29].

### F. Failure / escalation
- **Refer if:** no visible change after **4–6 months** of adherence; pigmentation darkening or spreading under treatment.
- **OTC ceiling (hard boundary):** deep/dermal pigment, widespread melasma, or any desire for faster clearing requires prescription hydroquinone, azelaic 15–20%, tranexamic acid, or procedures — outside OTC scope [S24][S29].
- **Red flags (immediate):** any new pigmented lesion with irregular borders/color/growth or a changing mole — skin-cancer evaluation, never "brighten" it [S29 clinical context].

## 4. Additional common conditions

## 4.1 Mild dry skin / eczema support

### A. Plain language
Dry skin = impaired barrier (lipid + water loss); eczema (atopic dermatitis) is a chronic barrier-defect + inflammation condition with itch-scratch cycles. **Chronicity: eczema is managed for life — emollients continuously, steroids only in flares** [S32].

### B. OTC ladder
| Active | Mechanism | Evidence | Concentration | Notes | Price tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ceramide-dominant moisturizer | Restores barrier lipids (ideal ~50% ceramides / 25% cholesterol / 15% fatty acids concept); cuts TEWL | Moderate–strong (ceramide-dominant regimen safely improved AD signs and restored permeability) [S30][S31] | n/a | Cream/ointment, fragrance-free; NEA Seal of Acceptance helps screening [S30] | Budget–derm-brand |
| Colloidal oatmeal 1% | Barrier support, anti-itch, pH buffering | Moderate (1% oat cream cut EASI ~51% in 14 days; systematic review supports as adjunct) [S36 ⚠][S37 ⚠] | 1% cream/soak | Adjunct; AAD endorses moisturizer category broadly, not specific actives [S32] | Budget |
| Hydrocortisone 1% | Mild topical steroid for flares | Standard of care at low potency; **label-limited** [S33][S34][S35] | 1% max OTC | Thin layer 1–2x/day on flare patches only, **max 7 days per label**; ~2 weeks is the practical dermatology ceiling `⚠ practice-level, not label` | Budget |

### C. Protocol
Lukewarm short showers/baths → apply moisturizer **within minutes of bathing ("soak & seal"), 2–3x daily** [S32]. Hydrocortisone 1% only on active itchy patches during flares, ≤7 days, never on infected-looking skin [S34][S35].

### D. Timeline
Itch/dryness improve within days–2 weeks of consistent emollients [S30][S32].

### E. Maintenance
Continuous emollients indefinitely; hydrocortisone reserved for flares ≤7 days per course [S32][S33].

### F. Failure / escalation
**Refer if:** no improvement in **7 days** of OTC hydrocortisone (label rule) [S34][S35]; repeated flare cycles needing steroid. **Red flags (immediate):** weeping/crusting/pain/spreading (infection — eczema herpeticum risk), widespread redness with fever, child <2 years needing steroid (ask doctor per label) [S34][S35].

## 4.2 Razor bumps (pseudofolliculitis barbae, PFB)

### A. Plain language
Curly hair cut short re-curves into the skin → foreign-body inflammation → papules/pustules along the beard. **Chronicity: effectively curable by stopping close shaving; chronic while shaving continues** [S38][S39].

### B. OTC ladder
| Intervention | Mechanism | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shaving technique (primary) | Prevents hairs re-entering skin | **Strong/authoritative** — AAD protocol [S38] | See C |
| Stop shaving / grow beard | Hairs grow out; bumps resolve | Strong (definitive per AAD/DermNet) [S38][S39] | Expect a bump surge for weeks 1–4 of regrowth [S38] |
| Salicylic acid / glycolic acid 1–2% | Exfoliate pore opening, free trapped hairs | Weak (expert recommendation, small studies) [S40 ⚠] | Adjunct, not substitute for technique |
| BP wash 5% (off-label adjunct) | Reduces secondary inflammation/infection | Weak (expert recommendation) [S40 ⚠] | Use if pustules |

### C. Protocol (AAD) [S38]
Shave at end of shower or after 5-min warm compress → apply moisturizing shaving cream, leave 1–2 min, keep wet → sharp single-blade or electric razor with guard → **shave with the grain, short strokes, never over an area twice, never stretch skin, no plucking ingrowns** → rinse warm, cool compress 5 min → soothing aftershave; shave daily or every 2–3 days (not less often); dry razor stored dry.

### D. Timeline
After stopping shaving: fewer bumps ~1 month, clear ~3 months [S38]. With technique-only: gradual over 4–8 weeks.

### E. Maintenance
Technique is permanent; or convert to beard [S38][S39].

### F. Failure / escalation
**Refer if:** persistent bumps despite correct technique (permanent grooves/scarring risk) [S38]. **Red flags:** abscesses, draining nodules, keloid formation, widespread scarring — dermatologist; **laser hair reduction (Nd:YAG for darker skin) is the most effective lasting option and the standard referral** [S39][S40 ⚠].

## 4.3 Chapped lips (incl. lip-licking cycle)

### A. Plain language
Lips lack sebaceous glands → water loss + wind/cold/licking strips them. Lip-licking dermatitis is a self-perpetuating cycle: saliva enzymes digest the lip barrier → drier lips → more licking, with a perioral irritant ring [S42]. **Curable in weeks if the habit and barrier are fixed; recurs seasonally.**

### B. OTC ladder
| Active | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| White petrolatum ointment | Strong (AAD first-line; seals water longer than waxes/oils) [S41] | Apply thick, frequent |
| Ceramides / shea butter / dimethicone balms | Moderate (recommended soothing barrier ingredients) [S41][S42] | For daily maintenance |
| Lanolin (occlusive, milk-fat derived) | Weak–moderate `⚠ allergy/sensitization potential in eczema-prone users not verified here` | Alternative occlusive |
| SPF lip balm | Standard adjunct for outdoor exposure [S42] | UV worsens cheilitis |
| **Avoid:** menthol, camphor, fragrance, flavoring balms | AAD guidance (irritants) [S41] | Common "tingling" balms worsen it |

### C. Protocol
Bland petrolatum balm applied frequently (after eating/drinking, before bed, before cold/wind exposure); break the licking habit (awareness + balm as substitute); hydrate adequately [S41][S42].

### D. Timeline
Comfort improves in 2–7 days; deep cracks/fissures heal over 2–3 weeks if protected from licking.

### E. Maintenance
Balm during dry/cold seasons and before outdoor exposure; SPF balm outdoors year-round [S41][S42].

### F. Failure / escalation
**Refer if:** no improvement after **2–3 weeks** of strict barrier care, or persistent perioral rash (consider contact allergy/perioral dermatitis). **Red flags:** recurrent splitting at mouth corners with redness (angular cheilitis — often candidal, needs treatment), crusted spreading lesions.

## 4.4 Mild rosacea support (OTC = supportive only)

### A. Plain language
Rosacea is a **chronic medical condition** (vascular + inflammatory) causing central-facial redness, flushing, sensitivity, sometimes papulopustules and eye irritation. **Not curable; requires medical diagnosis — OTC products manage comfort and triggers only. The assistant must route any suspected rosacea to diagnosis.** [S43]

### B. OTC-supportive ladder
| Intervention | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fragrance-free gentle cleanser + barrier moisturizer (ceramides, niacinamide, hyaluronic acid) | AAD self-care guidance [S43][S44] | "Fragrance-free" not "unscented" |
| Mineral (zinc oxide/titanium dioxide) SPF | AAD guidance; UV is a top trigger [S43] | Daily, always |
| Azelaic acid 10% OTC | Weak–moderate at OTC strength; validated rosacea strength is Rx 15% (Finacea) [S24] | Supportive for bumps/redness appearance |
| Trigger identification | AAD guidance [S43] | Sun, heat, alcohol, spicy food, stress diary |

**Avoid (AAD list):** alcohol, camphor, fragrance, glycolic acid, lactic acid, menthol, sodium lauryl sulfate on the face [S44].

### C. Protocol
Gentle AM/PM routine (cleanse, moisturize, AM mineral SPF); "sandwich" any active with moisturizer if stinging; keep a trigger diary [S43].

### D. Timeline
Barrier comfort/sensitivity improves in 1–2 weeks; underlying redness/lesions will **not** clear OTC — that needs prescription therapy.

### E. Maintenance
Continuous gentle routine + SPF + trigger management, indefinitely.

### F. Failure / escalation — **referral boundary is structural, not timeline-based**
Refer for diagnosis and treatment if: any suspected rosacea pattern (flushing + central redness, papules/pustules); ocular symptoms (gritty, red, watery eyes — risk to eye health); burning/stinging pain; rhinophyma (nose thickening); or no response to gentle care after 2–4 weeks [S43].

## Protocol honesty rules (assistant behavior)

1. **Manage-not-cure statements are mandatory** for chronic conditions: dandruff/SD (relapse at 3–8 weeks if treatment stops; maintenance is permanent) [S14][S19]; acne (chronic/relapsing; stopping treatment = recurrence) [S1]; melasma (no cure; lifelong photoprotection) [S29]; rosacea (medical, not curable, needs diagnosis) [S43]; eczema (emollients for life, steroids only in flares) [S32].
2. **Purging-vs-failure distinction** must be stated for every cell-turnover active (adapalene, retinol, AHAs): purge = more small lesions in weeks 2–6 within existing acne zones, stabilizing by ~6–8 weeks; failure = new lesion types/spread or no stabilization by week 12. Never tell a user to push through pain or worsening irritation [S5].
3. **OTC ceilings must be stated, not hidden:** azelaic 10% OTC ≠ 15–20% Rx evidence tier [S24]; ketoconazole 1% US-OTC ≠ 2% strength [S45][S46]; OTC brightening cannot reach deep dermal pigment [S29]; scalp psoriasis beyond mild scale is out of OTC scope [S17][S18].
4. **No cure language** for any antifungal, acne, or pigment product; "clears/controls/maintains," never "cures."
5. **SPF is a non-optional step** in every acne, PIH, melasma, and rosacea protocol; for melasma/PIH it must be tinted with iron oxides, not untinted [S22].
6. **Timelines are ranges with adherence conditions** ("6–8 weeks if used daily as directed"), never guarantees; if the user reports <2 weeks of use, the honest answer is "continue, judge at 6–8 weeks" [S3].
7. **Concentration claims must match the product label** shown to the user; market-specific legality (ZPT banned in EU/UK cosmetics since 2022; ketoconazole 2% P-only in UK, Rx in US) must be filtered per user market before recommending [S16][S45][S46].
8. **Never recommend** combining hydrocortisone with acne/brightening actives, using steroid courses >7 days, or "spot-testing" protocols for suspected infections/rosacea — refer instead [S34][S43].
