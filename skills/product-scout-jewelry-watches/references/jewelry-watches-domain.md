# Jewelry & Watches — domain reference

Family header: this file holds the jewelry/watches numeric tables,
worked examples, and per-sub-domain detail. Nothing operative lives
here — every walk-away trigger, disclosure duty, value-tier rail, and
verification rule is in the SKILL.md main file; on any disagreement,
the skill wins. Verification tags: `[S]` = sourced — verified against a regulator, standards body, official/primary source, or a family-verified precedent this cycle (inline `[S: URL]` carries the source); `[P]` = practitioner/market consensus or street anchor — labeled as such, never presented as sourced fact; `[A]` = anchored rail — a calibrated threshold set at install and re-anchored on a ~6-month cadence. Suffixes in any order: a source number (`[S13]`), a `YYYY-MM` date (mandatory on `[A]`), and lowercase modifiers — `unverified` (secondary coverage only; re-verify or drop before stating as fact), `industry` (vendor/marketing source), `convention` (usage convention, not evidence). A `[S, unverified]` claim is never the sole basis for a dosage or contraindication statement.

## 1. Karat and fineness conversion table `[S]`

| Mark | Fineness | Gold content | Typical regional use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 999 / 24k | 999 | 99.9% | Bullion coins, bars, East Asian & ceremonial pieces |
| 916–917 / 22k | 916 | ~91.6% | South Asian & Gulf jewelry standard |
| 875 / 21k | 875 | 87.5% | Widely used across MENA markets |
| 750 / 18k | 750 | 75.0% | European fine jewelry, gemstone settings |
| 585 / 14k | 585 | 58.5% | North American daily-wear standard |
| 375 / 9k | 375 | 37.5% | UK/Commonwealth budget gold — lower purity, higher durability |

Notes: "karat" (or "carat" in UK English) for gold fineness; "carat"
= gemstone weight (0.2 g) — never interchangeable.

## 2. Hallmarking systems — global overview `[S]`

- Mandatory assay/hallmarking countries (e.g., UK Assay Offices,
  Vienna Convention signatory states, India BIS Hallmarking, France,
  and many national assay laws) require precious metals to carry
  registered fineness + assay/sponsor marks before retail sale.
- Self-marking jurisdictions (e.g., US under FTC 16 CFR Part 23)
  require a maker's trademark alongside any karat quality mark.
- Fraud note: "correct stamp, wrong karat" fraud exists globally —
  the itemized receipt (weight, karat, gram price, making fee) is the
  enforceable record.

## 3. FTC metal-marking terms (16 CFR Part 23) `[S]`

| Term | Legal minimum under the FTC Guides |
|---|---|
| "Gold plate / gold plated / gold electroplate" | 10k-minimum fineness gold, ≥0.175 micron thickness (2018/2020 revision unified the two terms) |
| "Heavy gold electroplate" | ≥2.5 microns of 10k-minimum gold |
| "Gold flashed / gold washed" | Electrolytic 10k-min gold, NO minimum thickness — may never be called plate/electroplate |
| "Vermeil" | Sterling silver base + gold layer ≥2.5 microns, 10k-min fineness |
| "Sterling silver" / 925 | 92.5% silver |
| "Coin silver" | 90% silver |
| "Platinum" unqualified | Must meet the high fineness (950/900/850 group with platinum-group alloys) |

Treatment/lab-grown disclosure duties: §23.24 (all value-affecting
gemstone treatments disclosed), §23.12 (man-made products cannot use
"real, genuine, natural, artificial" misleadingly; lab-grown diamonds
are diamonds but origin must be disclosed).

## 4. Gold pricing formula `[P]` (fetch spot & local rates at dispatch)

Formula (main behavior 1): `price = (weight × today's local gram price for karat) + making fee + local tax/assay fee`.

- Convert global spot ounce price (1 troy oz = 31.1035 g) × purity
  fraction (e.g., 0.750 for 18k, 0.875 for 21k, 0.916 for 22k) into
  the user's local currency, or fetch the local gold-trade benchmark
  at dispatch.
- Making fees vary by region and complexity: machine-made chains at
  the low end, hand-worked bridal or designer jewelry at the top.
- Retail buyback pays metal weight at that day's buy rate — making
  fees and retail sell/buy spreads are lost immediately on resale.

## 5. Diamonds — 4Cs buyer-priority reference `[S]` unless noted

| C | Scale | Buyer translation |
|---|---|---|
| Cut | Excellent → Poor (GIA) | The sparkle driver; priority #1 at any budget |
| Color | D (colorless) → Z (light tint) | Adjacent letters differ little naked-eye; G-H is the common value zone [P] |
| Clarity | FL → I3 | "Eye-clean" ≈ VS2/SI1 and up to most eyes naked-eye [P]; the letters matter for paperwork |
| Carat | Weight (1 ct = 0.2 g) | Steps, not linear pricing: 1.00 ct disproportionately more than 0.90 ct |

Buying order (main behavior 5): cut first, then color/clarity at the
eye-clean/eye-white point. Fluorescence: usually inert to appearance
— do not pay premiums for or against it without the certificate line
[P].

## 6. Lab-grown vs natural — labs and methods `[S]`

- GIA (late 2025 onward): Laboratory-Grown Diamond **Quality
  Assessment** — "Premium" or "Standard" — no D–Z/FL–I3 grades for
  colorless lab-grown (>95% of lab-grown sit in a narrow band, GIA's
  stated rationale). Colored lab-grown reports still carry detailed
  grading. Older GIA LGDR (Laboratory-Grown Diamond Report; full
  grades) reports still circulate for stones graded before the change.
- IGI: continues full D–Z color + clarity grades for lab-grown — a
  real inter-lab difference to explain, not an error to correct.
- Growth methods: CVD (chemical vapor deposition, wafer-grown) and
  HPHT (high-pressure/high-temperature, press-grown) — both produce
  diamond; method disclosure on reports; treatment within lab-grown
  (post-growth HPHT color) also disclosable.
- Value profile [P 2026-11 — press-cited estimates]: retail prices 60–85%
  below natural equivalents; reported production-price collapse 80%+
  over recent years; resale near zero. Natural: retains more (not
  all) value. Neither is an investment (main behavior 13).

## 7. Gemstone treatments — by stone `[S]` (FTC §23.24 duties)

| Stone / treatment | Status | Buyer note |
|---|---|---|
| Sapphire, ruby — heat | Accepted, permanent, common | Must be disclosed; priced as heat-treated |
| Tanzanite — heat | Universal, permanent | Disclose; permanent color |
| Emerald — oiling/resin filling | Near-universal | Must disclose; care: no ultrasonic, no solvents |
| Diamond — laser drilling | Permanent | Separate disclosure from fills |
| Diamond — HPHT color | Permanent | Disclose |
| Diamond/gem — fracture/cavity filling | NOT permanent | Degrades with heat/UV/sunlight/repair; classic overpricing fraud vector |
| Stones — dyeing, coating, irradiation | Varies | Each requires separate disclosure |
| Pearl — dyeing | Common (freshwater) | "Tahitian" at freshwater prices = red flag |

"Untreated" premium claims: get in writing on the invoice (main
behavior 7).

## 8. Nickel and metals for sensitive skin `[S]` / `[P]`

| Metal | Nickel risk | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Implant-grade titanium, niobium | Effectively none `[S]` | The body-jewelry standards |
| 950 platinum | Very low | Premium price |
| 18k+ gold (palladium-white) | Low | Nickel-white gold alloys exist — ask `[P]` |
| Implant-grade stainless steel | Low (release below limits) | ASTM F136/F138 context for implants `[P]` |
| 925 sterling | Low (silver-copper alloy) | Coin-silver/low-fineness variants vary `[P]` |
| "Nickel silver" / German silver | HIGH | Contains nickel, zero silver |
| Unnamed "metal alloy" fashion pieces | HIGH | The default cheap-jewelry trigger |
| Thin-plated fashion | HIGH when coating wears | Base metal exposed at wear points |

EU REACH Annex XVII entry 27: release <0.5 µg/cm²/week (prolonged
contact), <0.2 µg/cm²/week (piercing posts); EN 1811 test method
`[S]`. Enforcement varies outside the EU — material choice is the
reliable control.

## 9. Pearls `[S]` / `[P]`

- Three classes: natural / cultured / imitation. Four cultured types
  (GIA): Akoya (small, white, high luster), South Sea (large,
  white/gold), Tahitian (dark, 8–16 mm), freshwater (affordable,
  varied shapes).
- Imitations: shell bead + coating (the "Majorica-style" product
  class `[P]`); legitimate at imitation prices.
- Mislabeling pattern: dyed freshwater sold as "Tahitian" `[S 2026-11 — GIA
  buyer guidance]`.
- In-store screens `[P]`: tooth test (gritty = nacre, smooth =
  imitation), luster depth, too-perfect surfaces, weight. GIA pearl
  identification report for valuable pieces `[S]`.
- Stranding: silk knotting between pearls = quality; gaps on stretch
  cord = fashion product `[P]`.

## 10. Watches — movements, certification, water resistance

Movement comparison `[S]` unless noted:

| Attribute | Quartz | Mechanical (hand-wound) | Automatic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power | Battery (~2+ yrs) | Daily winding | Rotor, wears off in ~2 days unwound `[P]` |
| Accuracy | Seconds/month | Seconds/day | Seconds/day |
| Service | Battery change | ~5–8 yr service cycle `[P]` — real cost | Same, plus possible rotor service |
| Buyer fit | Daily wear, gifts | Collector/romance | Collector + daily-wear bridge |

- COSC chronometer `[S]`: mechanical average −4/+6 s/day (uncased
  movement, 15 days, 5 positions, 3 temperatures); "Excellence"
  tier ≈ −2/+4 s/day announced; quartz standard ~±25 s/YEAR — a
  different certification, not comparable. "Chronometer" on a cheap
  dial is decoration.
- Water resistance `[P]` (industry bands): 3 ATM = splash only; 5 ATM
  = brief immersion at most; 10 ATM ≈ swimming; diving = dedicated
  ratings (ISO 6425 context) and service history. Ratings assume new
  gaskets; age voids the paper number.
- Microbrands vs legacy `[P]`: microbrands = spec-per-price, thinner
  service/resale; legacy = brand premium, service network, resale
  liquidity. Honest axes; verdict names the axis.

## 11. Luxury-watch authentication checklists

What the SKILL can check (main behavior 12) — the documented,
free checks `[S]`:

1. Price realism vs the market (highest signal — far-below-market
   "new" pieces = counterfeit or stolen).
2. Serial/reference consistency: case ↔ papers ↔ warranty card ↔
   seller photos; documented mismatch tactic.
3. Stolen-register check for pre-owned (The Watch Register class).
4. Channel: authorized dealer / established specialist with returns.
5. Buyer-visible physicals: engraving quality, crown action feel.

What requires a certified appraiser / brand service center: movement
inspection, case-back opening, provenance beyond papers. The skill
routes; it never certifies.

| Product | Safe on | Never on |
|---|---|---|
| Ammonia-based gold cleaner | Solid gold | Treated stones, pearls, emeralds |
| Ultrasonic cleaner | Solid gold/diamond (untreated) | Filled stones, emeralds, pearls, tanzanite |
| "Dip" cleaners | Gold/silver metals | Soft/porous stones (turquoise, opal, pearls) |
