## Spec-inflation table — power tools `[S]` unless noted

| Marketing claim | Reality | What to check instead |
|---|---|---|
| "20V MAX" / "MAX voltage" | Peak/startup convention, not sustained pack voltage (18V-class) | Compare within one brand's naming convention; sustained voltage is 18V-class `[S]` (tool-press documented convention) |
| "Peak horsepower" | Instantaneous startup draw — a repeatedly litigated spec-inflation pattern in consumer power equipment (peak-HP class actions against Stanley Black & Decker/Craftsman, DeWalt, Shop-Vac, Emerson) `[S]`; no settled damages figures are cited here | Measured cuts per charge, independent tests `[S]` |
| Stall/max torque headline | Momentary stall reading, not useful work output | Fastening tests, review torque curves `[P]` |
| Amp-hours as power | Ah = runtime per charge, never torque/power | Ah for runtime sizing, platform for power `[S]` (battery-system convention) |
| Corded wattage | Input draw, not output | cuts-per-minute, build quality, tests `[P]` |
| "Brushless" on no-name tools | A label without test coverage | Independent review or treat as unverified `[P]` |

## Cordless platform comparison — structure `[S]` / figures `[P]`

The major platforms (DeWalt 20V MAX, Makita LXT 18V, Milwaukee M18,
Bosch 18V, Ryobi ONE+, economy lines) are proprietary ecosystems —
batteries and chargers do not cross brands (verified market structure;
DeWalt sells one official 20V MAX→older-18V adapter). Evaluation
structure, in order:

1. **Starter-package price** (bare tool + 2 batteries + charger) vs
   bare-tool price — the honest comparison line `[P]`.
2. **Bare-tool lineup breadth**: drills, grinders, sanders, blowers,
   saws, garden tools available in the same platform locally — the
   3-year ecosystem test `[P]`.
3. **Local battery availability + price**: a platform whose packs are
   scarce or overpriced locally strands the toolkit — check verified
   local dealers before ranking `[P]`.
4. **Battery Ah ladder**: 2 Ah light tools, 4–5 Ah drills/grinders,
   6+ Ah mowers/saws `[P]`.
5. Cross-brand adapters exist (street price class ~$13–18 `[P 2026-11]`) — warranty/BMS caveats
   in SKILL.md behavior 3; never high-draw tools `[S]`.

Market note `[P]`: budget/economy lines dominate entry prices in many
markets; judge them as occasional-use class with shorter warranty terms
— not as pro-platform substitutes.

## Paint — base types and VOC bands

- Water-based (acrylic/emulsion/latex): low VOC, soap-water cleanup,
  fast dry, less yellowing — interiors default `[S]` (retailer/coating
  guidance).
- Oil/alkyd: hard enamel-like finish, moisture-resistant, yellowing
  risk, solvent cleanup — trim/wood/high-moisture `[S]`.
- Water-based acrylic-alkyd hybrids: oil-like durability, low VOC,
  non-yellowing — the trim middle path `[S]`.
- "Low-VOC" bands `[S]`: roughly <50 g/L for flat finishes and
  ~100–150 g/L for non-flat across common standards (Green Seal's
  current edition: 50/100; older editions and other schemes run
  higher) — the label number on the can is the operative figure.
- Primer logic `[P]`: bare plaster/drywall → alkaline-resistant/PVA
  primer; wood → wood primer; stains → stain-blocking; metal →
  anti-rust primer. Skipping primer = the #1 peeling cause.
- Sheen practice `[P]`: matte hides wall flaws, eggshell/satin for
  washable rooms, semi-gloss trim/wet areas.

## Ladders — selection and duty ratings `[S]`

ANSI/OSHA duty-rating classes (rider weight + tools + materials; EU
markets use EN 131 with 150 kg max load):

| Type | Rating | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Type III | 200 lb (~90 kg) | light household only |
| Type II | 225 lb (~102 kg) | household |
| Type I | 250 lb (~113 kg) | heavy household/trades |
| Type IA | 300 lb (~136 kg) | trades |
| Type IAA | 375 lb (~170 kg) | special duty |

The operative ladder rails — duty-rating selection, the 4:1 rule,
3-point contact, top-step and ground rules, fiberglass-near-power —
live in SKILL.md behavior 10. This table supplies the rating numbers.
Fiberglass ladders for any electrical-adjacent work — never metal
ladders near power lines `[S]` (OSHA/industry guidance).

## Hot-climate plant examples `[S]` species-level

For hot/arid climates (match to the user's hardiness zone for temperate
or cold climates):

- **Bougainvillea** — full sun, drought-tolerant once established.
- **Oleander** — extremely heat/drought tolerant, coastal-ok; TOXIC
  in all parts (SKILL.md behavior 12 rail governs where kids/pets
  share the space).
- **Lantana** — full sun, 6+ h direct sun, long blooming.
- **Pentas** — heat-loving perennial, sun/semi-shade.
- **Jasmine** — tolerates heat with deep regular watering.
- **Hibiscus** — reliable summer bloomer, afternoon shade helps.
- **Cacti/succulents and Mediterranean herbs** (rosemary, lavender)
  — water-wise options.

Practice `[P]`: deep/infrequent watering once established, heavy
mulch before peak summer, transplant in mild spring/autumn windows,
never mid-summer or deep frost; pots need ~2× the water of ground
plantings in peak heat.

## Per-project minimum kits `[P]`

Practitioner kit tables — project class first, then the minimum that
does it well. Verify against the user's stated project each time.

- **Hang shelves/pictures (masonry walls)**: hammer drill (or hammer-
  drill function), masonry bits, wall plugs matched to base, level,
  tape, eye protection. Concrete anchors for heavy loads. (For
  drywall: stud finder + toggle/hollow-wall anchors.)
- **Furniture assembly / wood repair**: cordless screwdriver or
  impact driver, bit set, wood glue (PVA), clamps, sandpaper set,
  hand saw.
- **Paint a room**: rollers + tray + extension pole, angled brush for
  edges, primer per surface, drop cloths, masking tape, FFP2/N95 mask
  (sanding), ventilation plan.
- **Fix a dripping tap**: adjustable wrench, replacement washer/
  cartridge (take the old one), thread tape, main-supply plan.
- **Balcony garden**: pots with drainage, potting mix, climate-matched
  starts, watering can, slow-release feed, hand trowel.
- **Basic household tool kit (first home)**: claw hammer, screwdriver
  set, adjustable wrench, tape measure, level, pliers, utility
  knife, assorted quality fasteners, cordless drill + 2 batteries,
  eye protection — bought as quality singles beats one cheap
  all-in-one box.

## Tool quality inspection points `[P]`

- Chuck runout: spin a long bit in any video listing — visible wobble
  = cheap chuck or bent spindle.
- Gear housing: all-metal on drills/grinders signals lasting gears;
  all-plastic on a "pro-claimed" tool is a downgrade tell.
- Switch/trigger feel, guard fit, baseplate flatness (sanders/saws),
  cord strain relief (corded).
- Battery pack weight vs claimed Ah (suspiciously light 5 Ah packs =
  inflated cells) `[P]`.
- Serial/date code consistency tool↔box↔manual; Bosch Professional
  serials carry a date-code prefix; Bosch power-tool authenticity
  runs through the security hologram/Secure Code Label checked at
  protect.bosch.com (verified) `[S]`; Makita and other major brands
  verify through their official regional channels `[P]` — SKILL.md
  behavior 5 carries the operative rule.

## Sub-domain driver detail

- **Plumbing fittings** `[P]`: regional stock mixes BSP (taper/
  parallel), NPT, and metric threads; take the old fitting to the
  shop; a thread that starts is not a thread that seals. PPR/PEX/
  copper hidden-wall lines need proper tooling; visible cold-water
  fixes are the DIY boundary.
- **Locks** `[P]`: ANSI grade 1 > 2 > 3 for deadbolts; EN cylinder
  classes where published; bolt throw ≥ 25 mm; strike plate into
  frame; padlocks — hardened shackle, body-over-shackle protection.
  Smart locks → electronics skill.
- **Outdoor furniture materials** `[P]`: powder-coated aluminum
  (light, rust-immune), teak (durable, weather-grey), eucalyptus
  (cheaper hardwood, needs oiling), synthetic rattan (UV-stabilized
  only — cheap rattan crumbles in 1–2 summers of sun), cushions in
  solution-dyed acrylic/polyester (fade-resistant) vs dyed-after
  (fades). Steel frames rust at welds — check coating coverage.
  Cross-ref home-living for indoor furniture and cushions' fabric
  numbers.
- **Lawn care honesty** `[P]`: turf in hot/arid climates = high water
  demand; drought-tolerant ground cover and hardscape are the value
  answer; where turf exists, mow high (shade roots), water
  deep/infrequent (dawn), fertilize spring/autumn only.
- **Grill build** `[P]`: thick cast-iron/stainless grates, stable
  wide base, grease catch manageable, lid fit; gas — burner count vs
  actual cooking area; charcoal — airflow control and ash removal.
  BTU headlines are marketing (SKILL.md behavior 2's logic).
- **Pool sizing** `[P]`: above-ground steel-frame pools dominate the
  value market; filter+pump package sized to volume; chemical dosing
  per label with the storage rails in SKILL.md behavior 14.

## Consumable buying notes `[P]`

- Paint is priced per liter/gallon with huge brand spread; higher-
  coverage brands often win on cost-per-m² — compare coverage, not can
  price.
- Fasteners sold loose by weight at hardware stores are fine for
  general use; use branded, load-rated fasteners for structural
  hanging.
- Seeds/seedlings: local nurseries stock what actually survives the
  local climate — their seasonal stock is a better signal than
  generic imported seed packets.
