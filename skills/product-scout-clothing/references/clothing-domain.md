## 1. Fabric Science

### Cotton
- Combing removes ~15% of short, brittle fibers and aligns long ones; combed cotton is smoother, stronger, less prone to pilling, and shrinks/distorts less than carded. Yarn quality hierarchy: open-end < carded < combed. [S14][S15]
- "Long staple" = fibers >~32 mm (~1.25 in); Pima, Supima, and Egyptian cotton are extra-long-staple varieties. Best fabric is both long-staple *and* combed. [S15]
- Fabric weight (GSM): ~130–150 lightweight (breathable, hot climates, may be semi-sheer and less durable); ~160–190 mid-weight (everyday sweet spot for durability + print quality); 200+ heavyweight (structured, opaque, shape-holding). GSM alone does not equal quality — judge with yarn count, knit type, and finishing. [S16][S17]

### Merino wool (micron = average fiber diameter in microns)
- The micron system is the modern grading standard (replacing "blood grade"/spinning count). Common apparel bands: ultrafine ≤15.5 (some sources use <16.5), superfine 15.6–18.5, fine 18.6–20.5, medium 20.6–22.5. Below ~15.5 = premium next-to-skin; above 18.5 = more durable, better for outer layers. Band boundaries vary by source — classifications are not perfectly standardized (market guides use ultrafine ≤15.5–16.5; Woolmark's certification spec instead sets Ultrafine Merino at required max 17.5 µm mean fibre diameter). [S13][S3][S 2026-11 — Woolmark spec AK-3: https://www.woolmark.com/globalassets/_06-new-woolmark/_industry/certification/_old-certification/product-specifications/ak-3_merino_ultrafine_2016_edit_final.pdf]
- Wool garments: higher micron = coarser feel (prickle threshold roughly <18–19 for most skin) *(practitioner consensus)*.

### Linen & silk
- Linen quality follows staple length and spinning: wet-spun long-staple ("line") flax yields fine, smooth fabrics down to ~50–100 gsm; dry-spun short-staple is coarser/heavier (~150–250 gsm). [S46][S 2026-11 — Safilin flax-spinning methods: https://www.safilin.fr/are-there-several-methods-of-flax-spinning/?lang=en]
- Certifications: **European Flax™** guarantees fiber origin (grown in Europe) only; **Masters of Linen®** additionally guarantees spinning *and* weaving in Europe — the stricter label. Belgian Linen™ members are automatically Masters of Linen certified. [S45]
- Silk weight is measured in **momme (mm)**: 1 momme ≈ 4.335 g/m². 19–22 momme is the accepted garment-quality range (16–19 for fluid drape; 25+ heavy luxury). [S47][S 2026-11 — momme conversion: https://sensoire.com/what-is-momme/]

### Synthetics: the wicking reality
- Cotton is hydrophilic — it absorbs up to ~25% of its weight in water into the fiber core, feels wet, dries slowly (evaporative cooling when dry; clammy chill when soaked). Polyester/nylon absorb almost zero moisture: sweat is moved along fiber surfaces and between-fiber capillary channels, spreads thin, and evaporates fast. Wicking ≠ absorbing. [S18][S19]
- Engineered synthetics (shaped fibers/grooves) and hydrophilic-finish polyesters outperform plain weaves; cotton/poly bilayer knits can wick inward and dry outward — a functional blend pattern. [S19][S20]
- Elastane (spandex/Lycra): typically 2–5% for stretch comfort. It is the durability weak link — degraded by heat (dryers, hot wash), UV, chlorine, and mechanical stretch cycling; swimwear breaking force fell ~12% after 200 h and ~66% after 300 h of chlorinated-water + sunlight exposure in a 2024 Polymers study. For frequent pool use, polyester/PBT blends resist chlorine far better than nylon/Lycra. [S21][S22][S23][S24]
- Blend rule of thumb: natural fiber gives comfort/absorbency, synthetic gives shape retention, quick-dry, and abrasion resistance; every % of elastane added shortens the realistic service life *(practitioner consensus built on S21–S24)*.

### Hot-climate fabric logic
- Best performers: linen (open weave + wicking, the summer gold standard), loose-weave lightweight cottons (voile, muslin, gauze, poplin, seersucker), hemp; TENCEL/modal/viscose are soft wicking alternatives. Avoid tight knits (trap heat) and everyday polyester (holds sweat at skin). [S25][S26]
- Modest layered wear: multiple *loose* breathable layers create cooling air gaps; a thin moisture-managing base layer plus loose open-weave outer layers beats one tight heavy layer. [S25][S26]

### Greenwashing: what the labels actually certify
- **OEKO-TEX Standard 100**: the finished product's *chemistry only* — tested for regulated + non-regulated harmful substances (pesticides, heavy metals, formaldehyde, azo dyes). It says nothing about organic content or supply chain. [S10]
- **GOTS**: certifies the whole chain — organic fiber content (≥70% or 95% tiers), approved processing chemistry, social/labor criteria, audited chain of custody. A product can be OEKO-TEX certified without any organic fiber; GOTS products generally also meet comparable chemical limits. [S11][S12]
- Buyer rule: organic claim → GOTS; chemical-safety claim → OEKO-TEX. Uncertified "eco/natural/green" wording is marketing, not evidence. [S11]

## 2. Construction Quality Signals

### Stitch density (SPI — stitches per inch)
- 6–8 SPI: sparse; seam-popping risk, heavy fabrics only. 8–10: standard commercial garments. 10–12: normal quality. **12–14+: premium benchmark** (~30% greater seam strength vs basic low-density seams; some specs cite 12–16). 14–16: delicate fabrics and high-stress seams (armholes, waistbands). [S1][S2]
- Trade-off: too few stitches → seam failure; too many → needle perforations weaken the fabric. Higher SPI costs more (slower sewing, more thread) — it is a real quality spend. [S1]

### What listings/photos can reveal (photo-checkable)
- Stitch density and evenness along seams and hems (count stitches per 2.5 cm in a zoomed photo vs the SPI bands above). [S1]
- Seam puckering, wavy hems, uneven seam allowances, misaligned joins. [S33]
- Pattern matching: stripes/plaids aligned at side seams, center front, pockets — a direct factory-QC tell. [S33]
- Buttonholes: densely bound edges, no fraying, correct size for the button; buttonholes are a standard QC inspection point. [S33]
- Lining presence and whether it is listed in the composition; lining that shows or bulges past the shell is a defect class. [S33]
- Hardware branding: **YKK printed on zipper sliders** is a baseline reliability marker (premium garments upgrade to RiRi/Lampo); YKK also makes marked buttons/snaps. [S27][S28]
- Print/embroidery: registration (edges misaligned vs design), density of embroidery stitching. [S33]

### What needs the in-hand checklist
- Fabric hand feel, true GSM/weight, opacity stretch tests. [S16]
- Seam slippage (tug seams gently at a join), needle holes/damage, thread trimming, symmetry (collar points, sleeve lengths, pocket placement). [S33]
- Button anchoring (twist test), zipper smoothness through full travel, care/content label presence and stitching (a permanent stitched label is a baseline compliance signal). [S33]
- Chemical odor (indicates finishing/dye shortcuts), lining fabric quality, print crack/stretch behavior. *(practitioner consensus)*

## 3. Sizing & Fit — the #1 Online Failure Mode

### Why letter sizes fail
- There is **no legally enforced sizing standard** for apparel in the US or EU; every brand defines its own chart. [S34]
- Vanity sizing is measured, not anecdotal: Franz (2017) analyzed size charts of **54 US retailers** and found sizes inflated most in **moderately priced women's brands**. [S31] The same body measurements can be "size 8" in one brand and "size 12" in another. [S32]
- Men's sizing is comparatively stable because it encodes inches (chest/waist); women's numeric sizes bundle multiple body dimensions that do not scale together — one waist number cannot predict hip/thigh/rise. [S34]
- Fit drives returns: online apparel return rate ~24.4% in the US (~20% e-commerce overall; ~26% global apparel), and **~53% of apparel returns are size/fit-related** — the single biggest cause. [S35][S36]

### Evidence-based mitigation (the method)
1. Keep body measurements (chest/bust, waist, hip; inseam for pants; foot length+width for shoes) updated — measure at end of day. *(practitioner consensus)*
2. Ignore the letter/number on the tag; read the brand's **flat garment measurements** — pit-to-pit width and back length for tops, waist/hip/rise for pants — and compare to a well-fitting garment you own. [S37]
3. Aggregate "true to size / runs small / runs large" from verified reviews; prefer size-specific reviews over overall stars; treat a brand where many reviews say "sized up" as requiring an adjustment. *(practitioner consensus; fit-prediction tools exist and reduce returns, but data quality varies by retailer)* [S34]
4. Women: check cup/sleeve and rise separately — numeric size alone under-determines fit. *(practitioner consensus)*

### Kids' sizing
- Age labels are unreliable: children of the same age vary enormously; charts that pair each size with **height (cm) and weight** ranges beat age-only charts. European height-based sizing (size = height in cm) is generally more accurate than US age labels. [S37]
- Between two sizes during rapid growth (fastest ~3 months–3 years): size up — but buy for height, not age. [S37]

### Shoe sizing
- Full size = one barleycorn = ~1/3 in (~8.5 mm); US men's ≈ UK + 1; EU sizes are Paris points (~2/3 cm per size) with **no official cross-system standard** — published conversion charts disagree. [S38][S39]
- Brannock devices standardize length + width (US letters: A narrow → D standard → E/EE wide), but feet are 3D: Brannock misses foot volume, so identical Brannock readings can need different sizes; between widths, go wider for a thick/fleshy foot. [S40][S41][S39]
- **Last variance is the real problem**: each manufacturer's last (and each model within a brand) has its own length/width/volume proportions; the same stated size fits differently — brand-level and model-level fit reviews beat any conversion chart. [S39][S41]
- Practical rules: size for the longer foot; length up a half size before width up (width scales within the length system); UK letters ≠ US letters. [S40][S39]

## 4. Durability Assessment — the Standardized Numbers

- **Abrasion (Martindale, ISO 12947)**: cycles until wear against a standard abrasive. Bands: 10–15k light domestic; 15–25k general domestic; 25–40k heavy/commercial; 40k+ contract; 50k+ extreme. Apparel needs less than upholstery (~20k is already robust for garments). American equivalent test: Wyzenbeek. [S4][S5]
- **Pilling (ISO 12945-2, modified Martindale)**: grades 1–5 (5 = no pilling); half grades allowed; **4–5 is the quality target**. [S6][S7]
- **Colorfastness (ISO 105)**: washing/rubbing/perspiration use the grey scale 1–5 (grade 4+ = acceptable minimum; 5 = no change); light fastness uses the blue-wool scale 1–8 (≥4 typical for indoor wear). A "4" for washing and a "4" for light are measured on different scales. [S8][S9]
- **Care-label symbols (GINETEX / ISO 3758; US: ASTM D5489 under the FTC Care Labeling Rule, 16 CFR 423)**: five symbols — washtub (number inside = max wash °C), triangle (bleach: empty = allowed, crossed = never), square (drying; circle-in-square = tumble), iron (dots: 1 ≈ 110 °C, 2 ≈ 150 °C, 3 ≈ 200 °C), circle (professional cleaning). More dots = hotter. US garments require a permanent care label. [S48][S49][S50]
- **Shrinkage on first wash**: cotton 3–5% (more with hot wash/dry); wool knits 15–20% if it felts (superwash-treated ~1–2%); polyester <1%; linen 2–5%; rayon/viscose up to 15–20%. Knits shrink more and mostly in width; wovens mostly in length; most shrinkage happens on the first wash then stabilizes. [S51]
- A durable-garment listing worth its price will typically publish: fiber %, GSM, Martindale or pilling grade, and care symbols. The absence of *all* of these is itself information. *(synthesis of S4–S9, S51)*

## 5. Footwear Specifics

### Running/athletic shoes
- ACSM's criteria for a good basic running shoe: minimal heel-to-toe **drop** (≤6 mm preferred; traditional trainers run 10–12 mm), lightweight, neutral cushioning, flexibility. [S52][S53]
- **Neutral vs stability**: neutral = even cushioning, no medial support; stability = firmer medial posts for overpronators (low arches / visible pronation). Motion control = maximum correction. [S53]
- The evidence (BJSM): prescription of elevated, cushioned, pronation-control shoes is **not evidence-based** (Richards 2008 systematic review); heel-drop (0/6/10 mm) showed no significant injury-risk difference; soft-vs-hard midsole and motion-control findings are mixed — motion-control may help some runners in cushioned shoes (Malisoux 2016/2020). Practical consensus: **comfort-first selection, adequate load management and strength work matter more than shoe category.** [S54][S55][S56]
- Cushioning taxonomy (max-cushion / daily trainer / lightweight tempo / race) is industry convention, not a standard. *(practitioner consensus)*
- Running-shoe durability: midsole foam (EVA) loses resilience with mileage — replace around 500–800 km. *(practitioner consensus; no formal standard found)*

### Leather grades & construction
- Working hierarchy: **full grain** (unsanded, strongest, patinas) > **top grain** (lightly sanded/refinished, uniform, still good) > **"genuine leather"** (lower layers, heavily corrected; the term is legally meaningless marketing) > **bonded** (scraps + adhesive; peels and flakes — avoid for anything you want to last). Caveat: "grades" are marketing convention, not an official standard. [S57]
- Sole construction: **Goodyear welt** = durability + resoleability + best water resistance (the buy-for-decades benchmark); **Blake stitch** = sleeker/flexible, resoleable only with special machinery, less water-resistant; **cemented (glued)** = cheap, light, effectively not resoleable — once the glue fails the shoe is done. Check "welted" or "stitched" claims in listings; photos of a visible welt strip along the sole edge support the claim. [S58]
- **Arch support**: strong orthopedic evidence for universal arch-support claims is weak; comfort-based selection and, where needed, individually fitted orthoses are the defensible guidance. *(practitioner consensus aligned with S54–S56)*
- **School shoes** (durability criteria): genuine/leather or durable synthetic upper; reinforced rigid toe cap / rubber toe bumper; stitched or injection-moulded (not glue-only) construction; abrasion, flex, and tear resistance tested; closed, flat, protective shape per children's-footwear systematic review. [S59][S60]

## 6. Marketplace Traps & Red Flags

- **Dropshipping quality gap**: generic supplier/stock photos routinely misrepresent what ships — sellers themselves report received items as "night and day" vs the photos; tells are identical photos across many stores, no real brand content, and long shipping windows. [S61]
- **Fake/incentivized reviews**: the FTC's final rule (Aug 2024) bans fake reviews, buying/selling reviews, undisclosed incentivized reviews tied to positivity, and review suppression; civil penalties ~$53,088 per violation (2025). Estimates cited in coverage run **up to ~40% of online reviews fake** on some platforms. [S62] Detection heuristics: 5-star clusters in short windows, unverified purchases, recycled photos, uniform phrasing. *(practitioner consensus)*
- **Labeling as the legal floor** (missing = strong proxy for gray-channel goods):
  - US: Textile Fiber Products Identification Act 1960 + 16 CFR 303 — fiber content in approved generic names by predominance, country of origin, manufacturer name or RN number. [S63][S64]
  - EU: Regulation (EU) 1007/2011 — mandatory fiber-composition label in standardized fiber names, durable/legible/attached, in the member state's language; animal-origin non-textile parts disclosed. [S65][S66]
  - Other markets enforce equivalent national fiber-content and permanent care-label laws.
- "Premium/luxury-grade/heavyweight" wording **without a disclosed fiber-content %** is unverifiable marketing; a listing with no size chart and no fiber content combines two red flags. *(practitioner consensus built on S63–S66)*
- Brand impersonation: misspelled brand names, seller accounts with unrelated product histories, prices far below brand MSRP with new-stock claims. *(practitioner consensus)*

## 7. Buying Timing & Value

- Seasonal clearance cycles: winter apparel is most deeply discounted **4–6 weeks after winter peak**; summer clearance runs **late summer**. Markdowns deepen the longer out-of-season stock sits. [S67][S68]
- **Major sale-event quality skew**: UK consumer group Which? found **92% of 2023 Black Friday "deals"** were the same price or cheaper at other times of the year; nine of 10 in 2024; eight of 10 in 2025. Retailers inflate reference prices pre-event. Use a price-history tool before trusting any event discount. [S69]
- Fast fashion vs durable math: fast-fashion garments are worn an estimated **7–10 times** before discard (Ellen MacArthur Foundation 2017; clothing utilization down ~36% vs 15 years prior). A $25 item worn 8× costs $3.13/wear; a $50 well-made item worn 100× costs $0.50/wear. [S70]
- Second-hand role: US secondhand apparel grew 14% in 2024 — ~5× faster than the broader retail clothing market (ThredUp 2025 Resale Report, with GlobalData); global secondhand is projected to reach $367B by 2029. For durability strategy, secondhand is *durability arbitrage*: garments that survived years of wear have demonstrated quality. [S71]

## 8. Extra-Long-Staple Cotton Authenticity (Global Commodity Fraud Example)

- **Egyptian cotton authenticity**: the Cotton Egypt Association estimated **~90% of global "Egyptian cotton" supplies were fake (2016)**; the Welspun/Target scandal (~750,000 bed-sheet sets mislabeled) triggered the crackdown. The genuine certification is the **gold Egyptian Cotton™ trademark seal** managed by the CEA with Bureau Veritas testing/auditing (DNA/mineral-traceability verified). Cheap "1000 TC 100% Egyptian cotton" claims are a global red flag; even in-hand feel cannot distinguish fakes — only the seal and price realism help. [S73][S74][S75][S76]

## Price-Per-Wear Framework

**CPW = purchase price ÷ realistic wear count** (projected before buying; tallied after). Only meaningful for repeated-wear items, not one-off occasion wear. [S77][S78]

Realistic wear-count assumptions *(practitioner estimates — assumptions, not sourced data; adjust per user)*:

| Garment class | Realistic wears | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday shoes/boots (welted, resoleable) | 300–600+ | Spend up [S58][S77] |
| Outerwear/coats (worn a season yearly) | 150–300 | Spend up [S77] |
| Underwear/basics/socks (daily) | 100–200 cycles | Spend up — comfort + elastic durability [S21] |
| Jeans / workwear trousers | 150–300 | Spend up [S77] |
| T-shirts (mid/heavy GSM, combed) | 50–100 | Mid-tier sensible [S16][S17] |
| Activewear (poly/PBT) | 50–100 | Mid-tier; elastane % is the lifespan cap [S21][S23] |
| Swimwear (frequent pool) | 1–3 seasons (~100–300 pool-hours) | Buy chlorine-resistant, not premium Lycra [S22][S23][S24] |
| Kids' clothing (outgrown, not worn out) | sized by growth | Cheap is fine; height-based sizing [S37] |
| Trend/occasion pieces | <20 | Cheap is fine — CPW stays high regardless [S70][S77] |

Decision rule: estimate wears *first*; a higher price is justified only when wear count is high **and** construction signals (Section 2/4) support the longer life. Fast-fashion's 7–10-wear baseline is the counterexample that makes durable basics' math win. [S70]

## Red Flags Table

| Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| No fiber-content label/% disclosed | Legally mandatory across major markets — absence signals gray-channel goods [S63][S65] |
| No size chart, or chart without garment measurements | Fit is the top return driver; measurement-free charts predict failure [S35][S37] |
| "100% Egyptian cotton" at low price, no gold seal | ~90% of the global market was estimated fake (2016); verify the CEA/Bureau Veritas seal [S74][S75] |
| Only 5-star reviews, unverified purchases, recent clusters | FTC banned fake/incentivized reviews; up to ~40% estimated fake on some platforms [S62] |
| Stock-photo listing / same photos across stores | Classic dropshipping quality-gap tell [S61] |
| "Premium/heavyweight" with no GSM or fiber % | Unverifiable marketing — the numbers are the only proof [S16][S63] |
| GOTS/OEKO-TEX-style wording without certificate ID | Real certs carry license numbers; wording alone is greenwashing [S10][S11] |
| Bonded/"genuine" leather at premium price | Bonded peels; "genuine" is a marketing term, not a grade [S57] |
| Cemented construction sold as "forever" footwear | Glued soles are not resoleable [S58] |
| Event "50% off" without price history check | 8–9 of 10 Black Friday deals matched/beat at other times [S69] |
| High elastane % sold as long-life activewear/swimwear | Elastane degrades fastest — chlorine/heat/UV kill it first [S21][S22] |
| No care symbols or care label missing | Permanent care labels are required in major markets (e.g., 16 CFR 423) [S49] |

## Agent rules (do not edit this section)
1. Explicit consent before first save of new profile data; refusal remembered, never re-asked. The one-time move of an already-consented profile to the family's current storage location is not a new save — it is copied once, with a one-line notification, and never re-asks consent.
2. Garment-attribute descriptions only — raw photos are never persisted; no health data lives in this file.
3. One-command deletion: deleting the file erases everything; the memory pointer line is removed on request too.
4. Never re-ask a fact dated within 6 months; re-measure reminders only when the user mentions weight or fit changes.
5. A current-session user statement ALWAYS overrides any line above.

## Appendix — measurement protocol (the template of record; SKILL.md holds the operative rules)

How the tape-measure step works (walk the user through it in their
language): end-of-day measuring; chest/bust at the fullest point, waist at
the natural waist, hips at the widest; inseam along the inner seam of a
well-fitting pair; foot length heel-to-longest-toe on paper; note width
(feels pinched / standard / fleshy). If the user prefers not to measure,
accept a well-fitting garment's flat measurements instead — pit-to-pit
width and back length — which often beats body measurements anyway.
